## 0. Governing Orientation

This document exists to compile the current quadrant work from conceptual/philosophical form into a **substrate-independent functional contract** suitable for the next descent stage.

The governing orientation is therefore not “solve the metaphysics of holons, subjectivity, or reality.” It is:

> **Specify what a downstream SIGMA/ECOS system must preserve, distinguish, expose, and re-open so that quadrant inquiry can be used provisionally without encoding a known semantic defect.**

Register B closure is reached when the contract is sufficiently coherent, adversarially tested, transferable, and bounded to govern the next architecture/design step. Residual philosophical uncertainty may remain when it does not change the present downstream functional requirements and has an explicit reentry condition.

This contract deliberately preserves the distinction between:

- **semantic commitments** required for bounded operation;
- **working structural hypotheses** still being qualified;
- **anti-generators** already rejected as foundations for quadrant identity;
- **open questions** that would materially change the next descent stage;
- **nonblocking metaphysical questions** that are outside the present decision surface.

## 1. Core operational stance: no semantically monadic referents

### 1.1 Register B commitment

For SIGMA/ECOS operation, a stable referent identifier must never be treated as evidence that the referent is semantically atomic, self-contained, context-free, or exhaustively represented.

**Operational rule:**

> **Every admitted referent is handled as holonically situated and potentially compositionally incomplete.**

This is a system design commitment, not a metaphysical proof that reality is composed in one unique way.

A referent may be conceptually isolated strongly enough to receive a stable identity and become the focal object of inquiry. That individuation is a controlled mapping operation. It does not erase:

- its constituent relations;
- the larger contexts in which it participates;
- alternative valid boundaries under different Governing Orientations;
- unresolved or latent relations not yet surfaced;
- the possibility that future evidence requires re-individuation.

The system must therefore preserve the **scalpel of conceptual individuation** without confusing the cut with the territory.

### 1.2 Stable identity is continuity, not completeness

A stable ID provides continuity of reference across observations, claims, questions, representations, and changes of inquiry.

It does **not** establish:

- atomicity;
- ontological independence;
- complete individuation;
- complete constituent knowledge;
- complete containing-context knowledge;
- exhaustive relational knowledge;
- permanent boundary correctness.

A referent can remain the same referent while its current map of constituents, containing relations, or participation becomes richer or is corrected.

### 1.3 Under-specified referents are not rejected; they are exposed

When a referent arrives without enough constituent, containing, relational, temporal, or orienting information to support the intended inquiry, do not fabricate completion and do not silently treat it as monadic.

Treat it similarly to raw, unprocessed system input:

1. preserve the original input and source;
2. assign or resolve stable identity only to the degree warranted;
3. perform minimal mechanical normalization;
4. expose materially missing orientation or holonic context;
5. generate the **minimum sufficient set of specific, directive, calibrated questions** needed to expose and discriminate every materially distinct uncertainty branch that could change the current Move, its interpretation, the referent boundary, or the stop/fail condition;
6. stop adding questions when the remaining uncertainty is either nonconsequential under G, already discriminated by an existing question, or would not change the current Move; then continue only to the degree allowed by the Governing Orientation and current evidence.

A missing relation is recorded as **unknown / latent / unresolved**, not as absent.

## 2. Co-arising and the preservation of latent context

### 2.1 Co-arising as operational grammar

The current system imports **co-arising** as a functional principle:

> A focal referent, its constitutive organization, its participation in wider relations, and the four quadrant inquiry obligations are treated as jointly available aspects of one referential situation rather than as independent objects created on demand.

Conceptual isolation changes the map's focus, not the existence of the omitted relations.

### 2.2 Individuation must preserve return paths

Every focalization must retain enough recoverable information to return to the referent's deeper and wider context.

A downstream system must therefore preserve, directly or compositionally:

- what boundary is currently being used for R;
- why that boundary is consequential under G;
- what is presently treated as constituent of R;
- what is presently treated as co-relatum / containing context rather than constituent;
- what material boundary questions remain unresolved;
- what would trigger explicit re-individuation;
- links or questions that permit return to constituent and containing relations.

The purpose is not exhaustive enumeration. The purpose is **continuous recoverability of the fact that the current focal cut is partial**.

### 2.3 The latent / infinite is represented as open-world incompleteness

SIGMA/ECOS does not need to enumerate an infinite relational universe in order to remain in contact with it.

Operational contact with the latent/infinite is maintained through:

- open-world semantics: unrecorded does not mean nonexistent;
- explicit unknowns;
- calibrated Question Forward;
- preserved provenance and referent continuity;
- reentry conditions;
- the ability to discover new constituents, co-relata, or fields without rewriting history.

The Governing Orientation determines when enough has been disclosed for the current Move.

## 3. Governing Orientation is a prerequisite, not decoration

Quadrant inquiry without a sufficiently locked Governing Orientation is unsafe except when explicitly sandboxed as a boundary experiment.

For bounded operational use, G must supply or resolve enough of the following to orient the inquiry:

| Orientation function | Minimum requirement |
|---|---|
| **Decision focus** | What current decision, understanding, or Move is being served. |
| **Relevance** | What makes a fact, relation, question, or distinction consequential now. |
| **Discernment** | What kinds of differences matter to the current inquiry. |
| **Value / priority ordering** | How tensions or competing considerations are resolved when they matter. |
| **Ideal end state** | What a satisfactory outcome would look like at the current resolution. |
| **Sufficiency / stop condition** | What counts as enough understanding to proceed. |
| **Failure condition** | What invalidates the current traversal or blocks descent. |
| **Reentry condition** | What future evidence or contradiction requires reopening the inquiry. |

These need not exist as one giant declaration. They may be resolved compositionally from governing sources.

If the orientation changes materially during traversal, prior answers must retain their original scope and be requalified as needed.

## 4. First-class relational coverage

### 4.1 Four co-arising obligations

Every admitted focal referent exposes four co-arising quadrant inquiry obligations.

The current working umbrella concept is **Relational Face**: a single first-class fourfold relational inquiry structure associated with R under G.

The **concept** is stronger than the current name. Final terminology remains open.

The functional requirement is:

> **The four quadrant obligations must be structurally available and conspicuous even when one or more cannot yet be answered.**

A missing answer is not a missing quadrant. A blank cell with no question is insufficient. Material gaps should be converted into calibrated questions or explicitly marked nonconsequential under the current G.

### 4.2 Coverage is not epistemic completeness

A traversal can be complete enough for the current Move while some quadrant answers remain unknown.

The distinction is:

- **unconsidered quadrant** → structural coverage gap;
- **considered but unresolved quadrant** → legitimate epistemic openness;
- **considered and currently nonconsequential under G** → bounded deferral;
- **answered quadrant** → claim whose standing depends on warrant.

The system must not fabricate claims merely to populate all four positions.

## 5. Candidate generative distinctions

The quadrant grammar is currently modeled as two crossed binary distinctions. These are working structural descriptions, not final pole names.

### 5.1 Distinction A — constitutive seat / participatory seat

Earlier language: **Whole / Participation**.

This document sharpens that formulation because every referent is treated as compositionally situated and both sides are relational.

**Constitutive-seat inquiry** asks about R under the currently declared boundary: the distinctions, relations, constituents, organization, and conditions counted as part of R being this whole for the present inquiry.

**Participatory-seat inquiry** asks about the same R in relations that cross or exceed that current constituent boundary: co-relata, containing contexts, fields, exchanges, conventions, environments, systems, or other relations through which R participates.

The focal referent remains R on both sides.

The distinction is **not**:

- relational / nonrelational;
- inside / outside in a spatial sense;
- small / large;
- individual / collective;
- local / global;
- component / system in every case;
- isolated object / surrounding environment.

A change in what counts as constituent of R is **re-individuation or boundary revision**, not ordinary quadrant traversal.

**Qualified operational discriminator:** Recover the declared boundary and the relation an adequate answer must establish. Inquiry into R's constituent organization uses the constitutive seat; inquiry into R's specified relation with a co-relatum uses the participatory seat. Causal necessity, physical enclosure, scale, and access do not decide constituency. If the seat depends on an unresolved boundary choice, expose that choice and a discriminating Question Forward rather than assign a coordinate by convenience. Discovering a constituent already admitted by an unchanged boundary criterion can enrich the account; changing that criterion or converting an expressly excluded co-relatum into a constituent requires explicit revision.

### 5.2 Distinction B — governing possibility structure / determinate case

Working description:

**Possibility/dependency inquiry** asks what distinctions, conditions, criteria, or dependencies govern what may, must, or cannot be the case for the indexed inquiry.

**Determinate-case inquiry** asks what condition, configuration, state, change, relation, membership, event, or result is the case here under the declared scope.

The distinction is a difference in **question burden**, not sentence grammar or content type.

Therefore:

- a dependency can itself be the determinate fact under inquiry;
- a governing structure can imply only one admissible result;
- a determinate answer may require substantial interpretation;
- a possibility/dependency answer may be directly observable or mechanically derived;
- neither side owns facts, rules, meanings, observations, reports, experiences, or measurements as content classes.

The working plain-language discriminator is:

> **What governs what may or must be the case?**  
> versus  
> **What is the case here?**

**Qualified operational discriminator:** State what an adequate answer must do with the scoped subject matter. Identifying the dependency or configuration that obtains is a determinate-case burden; establishing what that dependency permits, requires, or excludes is a governing burden. The same content may serve each role. A compound question may require more than one obligation. Where ordinary language does not determine the answer burden, clarify or decompose the question; do not force a unique classification from words such as “rule,” “actual,” “possible,” or “defines.” Missing evidence can leave either answer unresolved.

This distinction is qualified only for provisional bounded use under ECO-143's tested envelope and reentry conditions.

## 6. Four-position functional contract

Crossing the two working distinctions yields the current quadrant obligations:

| Relational seat | Governing possibility structure | Determinate case |
|---|---|---|
| **Constitutive seat** | **UL candidate obligation:** What distinctions, constituent relations, criteria, capacities, or dependencies govern what R can be or do as this currently individuated whole? | **UR candidate obligation:** What properties, state, configuration, behavior, event, change, or other determinate condition characterizes R as this currently individuated whole? |
| **Participatory seat** | **LL candidate obligation:** What relations, rules, conventions, dependencies, compatibility conditions, mutual constraints, or field structures govern what can occur through R's participation with relevant co-relata? | **LR candidate obligation:** What relations, exchanges, memberships, transactions, couplings, positions, effects, or other determinate relational conditions obtain for R in that participation? |

These definitions must remain usable for:

- human referents;
- nonhuman referents;
- artificial systems;
- abstract/formal referents;
- distributed systems;
- bounded situations and episodes;
- composites whose constituent boundary is partly conventional or purpose-relative.

No class receives a special quadrant rule.

## 7. Phenomenology and first-person material: explicit non-dependence

Phenomenology has **zero generator weight** in this contract.

SIGMA/ECOS does not need to determine whether a referent possesses phenomenal consciousness in order to construct or classify a quadrant inquiry.

Operational requirement:

> **For a fixed structural question, focal referent, boundary, and scope, quadrant identity does not depend on phenomenal experience, subjectivity, or first-person ontology. G may make a claim about experience consequential and thereby select or change the question under inquiry; this does not authorize phenomenology-based quadrant assignment. A material change in G or question burden must be made explicit and affected answers requalified.**

If experience, intention, felt meaning, or first-person report is consequential, it becomes ordinary subject matter and evidence under the same structural grammar as any other phenomenon.

A report such as “I feel trapped” may support a determinate claim, a governing-condition inquiry, a constitutive-seat inquiry, a participatory-seat inquiry, or more than one—depending on the exact question and claim role.

No inference is licensed from “phenomenological” to any quadrant.

The human-experience case remains useful only as a regression specimen against the old mistake.

## 8. Evidence, access, and mapper posture

### 8.1 Evidence does not generate quadrant identity

The same evidence may participate in multiple quadrant inquiries.

Quadrant identity is determined by the structural relation the question asks the mapper to establish, not by:

- direct versus indirect access;
- observation versus inference;
- first-person versus third-person report;
- measurement versus interpretation;
- visibility;
- evidence format;
- storage location;
- mapper confidence.

### 8.2 Access and warrant remain separate

For every consequential claim, preserve:

- what evidence is available;
- how it was obtained;
- what it supports;
- what remains uncertain;
- what would falsify or revise the claim.

Missing evidence makes the answer unresolved. It does not remove the quadrant obligation.

### 8.3 “Take R's place” is an anti-projection cue

Useful operator cue:

> **Take R's place in the inquiry: preserve the distinctions, constraints, capacities, and relations warranted for R; do not lend it yours.**

This cue applies across all four quadrants.

It does not imply consciousness, simulation, empathy, or direct access. It exists to reduce mapper projection.

## 9. Minimal referent-orientation envelope

Before a quadrant traversal can be treated as functionally reliable, the system must have or resolve enough of the following:

| Element | Required standing |
|---|---|
| **R — focal referent** | Stable enough identity for the current Move. |
| **Boundary** | What currently counts as constituent of R and why. |
| **Holonic seat** | Known constituent relations and known containing/participatory relations, with gaps explicit. |
| **G — Governing Orientation** | Sufficient decision focus, relevance, priority, ideal end state, stop/fail/reentry conditions. |
| **Time / state scope** | Relevant temporal boundary where consequential. |
| **Relational scope** | Known relevant co-relata plus the criterion for admitting newly discovered ones. |
| **Evidence/access state** | Current sources, access routes, warrant, and material limitations. |
| **Coverage state** | Which quadrant obligations are answered, unresolved, deferred, or unconsidered. |
| **Question Forward** | A qualitatively sufficient set of directive calibrated questions for each material gap: enough to expose and discriminate every consequential uncertainty branch, but no more than needed once additional questions would not change the current Move. |

This is a **logical envelope**, not a requirement for one giant record or schema object.

## 10. Failure, refocus, and requalification conditions

A traversal must stop or explicitly requalify when any of the following occurs:

### 10.1 Referent substitution

The inquiry silently changes from R to:

- a constituent of R;
- a property or state of R treated as a new subject;
- a co-relatum;
- the containing field;
- the mapper;
- the representation of R.

If the new object should become focal, declare refocus and preserve lineage.

### 10.2 Boundary change

A relation previously treated as external to R becomes constituent, or vice versa, in a way that materially changes the inquiry.

Declare re-individuation or boundary revision. Requalify affected answers. Do not pretend this was ordinary quadrant movement.

### 10.3 Governing Orientation change

Purpose, relevance, value ordering, ideal end state, or stop/fail conditions change materially.

Preserve prior answers under their original orientation and requalify what becomes consequential.

### 10.4 Access/warrant collapse

The mapper confuses how something is known with what structural question is being asked.

Repair the claim or traversal; do not redefine the quadrant.

### 10.5 Anti-generator regression

The mapping falls back to a rejected generator such as subjective/objective, inferred/observed, hidden/visible, phenomenological/nonphenomenological, individual/collective, or small/large.

Treat this as grammar misuse or candidate failure depending on whether the confusion survives clarification.

### 10.6 Unsupported closure

A quadrant is populated with a plausible answer that outruns its evidence, or a missing quadrant is silently treated as irrelevant.

Convert the gap to Question Forward or explicitly defer it under G.

## 11. Known anti-generators

The following must not define quadrant identity:

| Rejected generator | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **Subjective / objective** | Imports consciousness and epistemic status as structural coordinates. |
| **Interior / exterior** | Carries spatial, phenomenological, and access baggage that does not generalize cleanly. |
| **Internal / external** | Useful as a thinking cue in some cases but semantically overloaded and boundary-dependent. |
| **Inferred / observed** | Describes evidence route, not inquiry target. |
| **Interpretive / observable** | Confounds method/access with structural obligation. |
| **Hidden / visible** | Visibility is contingent access. |
| **Individual / collective** | Fails for composites, abstractions, fields, and nonhuman systems. |
| **Small / large; local / global** | Turns relational index into scale. |
| **Phenomenological / nonphenomenological** | Content class is not a generator. |
| **Rule / fact as sentence type** | A rule or dependency can itself be the determinate case being asked about. |
| **Many possibilities / one actuality** | A governing structure may yield one admissible result. |

This table is not claimed exhaustive. Add a new anti-generator only when it produces a materially different downstream grammar or recurrent failure.

## 12. Question Forward contract for incomplete referents

When a referent or traversal is materially incomplete, the system must generate calibrated questions rather than generic “unknown” labels.

A useful Question Forward should include:

1. **specific missing relation or distinction**;
2. **why it matters under G**;
3. **what evidence or signal would answer it**;
4. **what contrasting findings would mean**;
5. **what downstream claim or Move would change**.

Examples:

- “Which components are actually required for this service to preserve identity under the current G, and which are merely coupled dependencies? A configuration/version boundary or failure-isolation test would discriminate them. If the database is constitutive rather than a co-relatum, the current Upper/Lower traversal must be re-individuated.”

- “Does this seam correspond to a load-bearing discontinuity or only a surface marking? Inspect seam movement under the stipulated load. If movement tracks the seam, the current possibility/dependency account changes; if not, the seam remains observationally real but may be nonconsequential under G.”

Question Forward is the mechanism by which bounded individuation remains in active contact with latent context without requiring exhaustive inquiry.

### 12.1 Question Forward sufficiency gate

A Question Forward package is **analytically sufficient** when:

- every material uncertainty that could change the current Move, interpretation, boundary, or closure condition is represented by at least one specific question;
- materially different possible answers are discriminable by an identified signal, observation, or evidence route;
- each question names the downstream claim, boundary, action, or reentry condition that would change;
- no consequential uncertainty is hidden merely because it is difficult to formulate;
- additional questions would be redundant, nonconsequential under G, or would not alter the present decision boundary.

The gate is therefore qualitative, not numerical. One well-formed question may be sufficient for a gap; a larger set may be required when distinct uncertainty branches would lead to different consequences.

## 16. Remaining consequential open questions

**Q2 — Possibility/dependency / determinate-case discriminator**

**Q3 — Transferability**  
Can a fresh worker preserve R, G, boundary, evidence standing, and the four obligations on unseen material without material repair to the contract?

**Q4 — Descent safety**  
Does any surviving uncertainty still threaten to change the design requirements that follow from this contract?
