## 4. Live failure specimen — referent-seating debt displacement

The session produced a high-value natural failure specimen.

### Initial condition

The Principal observed that generalized user-story structure looked like an established entry point into UL-relevant information, while functional requirements/specifications were already serving a useful LR-oriented sorting/addressing role in the current D&I organization.

### Failure

The worker correctly noticed that, in the abstract, a functional requirement is not universally identical with LR.

However, the worker silently changed the object of analysis from:

> “How functional requirements/specifications are currently organized and used in this ECOS/SIGMA work”

to:

> “What quadrant a functional requirement belongs to in a universal ontology.”

That correction was locally defensible but globally mis-seated.

The Principal noticed the mismatch and considered correcting it, but did not immediately intervene. The unresolved mismatch then became referent-seating / qualification debt.

The worker continued reasoning and silently swapped the focal object several more times in order to support successive claims. The prose remained internally coherent while different claims were being proven against different objects.

### Consequence

This is a direct specimen of debt displacement:

- an unresolved upstream mismatch can remain latent;
- local reasoning can stay polished;
- the debt resurfaces later at a less convenient point;
- downstream analysis may become substantially more tangled than the original correction would have been.

### Recovery

The Principal stopped the derivation and explicitly reseated the work.

Recovery pattern:

> low-pressure Sense → point of view → initial contrast → object of focus → containing whole / salient parts → general PGO → extended inference.

The process failure and the recovery are first-class architecture evidence, not secondary commentary.

## 6. User stories as an UL-facing elicitation technology

The session identified an established software-engineering practice with unusually strong structural relevance.

Classic user-story grammar:

> As a [user/role], I want [capability/outcome], so that [reason/value].

The useful generalization is not “user stories are UL.”

Rather:

> User stories are a mature elicitation / communication technology that often preserves focal-local objective, possibility, dependency, and intended-outcome information that functional requirements and specifications can suppress.

The important structural contribution is that the story form keeps the focal seat visible while exposing why a capability matters.

### Generalized focal-referent form

A first-order extension beyond human users:

> For focal referent R, condition/capability/dependency X matters because it enables, preserves, constrains, or avoids consequential possibility/outcome Y.

Anthropomorphic shorthand such as:

> “As a distributed password-management vault, I need established cryptography for easy authentication and verification”

may be useful for discovery even when the referent has no phenomenological “want.”

A non-anthropomorphic equivalent is:

> For the distributed vault, established cryptographic mechanisms are a dependency of the authentication/verification possibility structure relevant to the current objective.

### Why this matters

The story-like representation can retain information that a lower-level prescription may lose.

A specification can say what to implement while failing to preserve why that implementation is supposed to count as adequate.

This makes generalized user-story structure a promising UL-facing interrogation operator and a valuable source for structural generalization.

## 7. Quadrant coverage

Current strong working grammar for a fixed referent R under explicit scope:

- UL: focal-local possibility/dependency structure of R as a whole.
- UR: determinate state/change of R as a whole.
- LL: possibility/dependency structure of R in relevant relations.
- LR: determinate state/change of R in relevant relations.

Important caution:

- user stories, requirements, specifications, and artifacts are not quadrants;
- each can contain information that projects across more than one quadrant;
- the useful question is what information the artifact or operation preserves, suppresses, transforms, or exposes for the active envelope.

The generalized user-story form is presently valuable because it preferentially preserves focal-local objective / possibility / dependency information, not because all story content is structurally UL.

## 8. Ideal / instrumental / operational relationship — preserve, do not prematurely collapse

The session reopened the recurring relationship among:

- ideal enacted condition — what condition should ultimately obtain;
- instrumental relation — what capability, dependency, constraint, or arrangement matters because it helps produce/preserve that condition;
- operational enactment — what bounded action/realization must occur now to enact/test the instrumental relation.

Do not map these directly onto quadrants.

The current unresolved question is how this means–ends depth interacts with the focal objective neighborhood and quadrant disclosure while preserving the already-useful D&I roles of functional requirements and specifications.

Functional requirements are therefore not yet adopted as “the matching abstraction altitude” for UL.

Treat them as an existing intermediate design/implementation artifact whose exact structural job should be recovered in relation to:
- focal objective neighborhood;
- ideal / instrumental / operational organization;
- quadrant disclosure;
- D&I;
- specifications and acceptance behavior.
