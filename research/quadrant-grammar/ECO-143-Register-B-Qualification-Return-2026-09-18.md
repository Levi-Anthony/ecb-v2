## 2. Frozen operational candidate

**Fixed through an ordinary traversal:** focal R, G, consequential constituent boundary/individuation, time/state scope, and the declared relevance criterion. Known co-relata are an initial scope, not an exhaustive universe. New relevant relations may be disclosed; requalify if their discovery changes boundary, G, or previous answers.

**First distinction:** change the relational seat of the question. Constitutive inquiry asks about R as the presently bounded whole, including its constituent organization. Participatory inquiry asks about that same R through a specified relation crossing/exceeding that boundary. Both are relational. Neither physical enclosure, causal importance, spatial extent, nor singular/plural grammar determines the seat.

**Second distinction:** change what the answer must establish. One burden establishes conditions/criteria/dependencies governing admissibility, capacity, necessity, or impossibility for the indexed question. The other establishes which configuration, condition, relation, or dependency obtains in this scoped case. The same rule can be identified as the determinate item and then used to constrain what follows. A single admissible outcome does not erase that difference.

| Seat / burden | Governing possibilities or dependencies | Determinate case |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutive | UL: what governs what R can be/do as this bounded whole? | UR: what characterizes R as this bounded whole here? |
| Participatory | LL: what governs what can occur through R's specified participation? | LR: what obtains through that participation here? |

These labels are working coordinates, not final pole names.

**Operational discriminator for Q1:** recover the declared boundary and identify the relation the question requires an answer about. Is it R's constituent organization, or R-in-relation-to a co-relatum? Material dependence alone does not make something constituent. If the answer depends on an unresolved constituent/co-relatum choice, expose that choice and ask what source or test would settle its consequence under G. Do not choose a seat by proximity or convenience. A newly discovered member already admitted by an unchanged boundary criterion can enrich an account; converting something expressly outside that criterion into a constituent revises the boundary.

**Operational discriminator for Q2:** specify what an adequate answer must do with the scoped subject matter. Identify the installed/obtaining dependence? Determinate burden. Establish its implications for what may/must/cannot occur? Governing burden. “Which rule?”, “why?”, “actual”, and “possible” are insufficient lexical classifiers. A compound question may require both obligations; decompose it without forcing the evidence into one bin. Governing claims themselves still require warrant.

**Referent substitution:** changing from R to a part, property, experience, report, model, co-relatum, or containing system as the subject is refocus, with lineage preserved. Making a property salient as a predicate of R is not refocus.

**Evidence/access:** a source, observation, report, interpretation, or measurement can support several obligations. Changing its route can change warrant while leaving the question's coordinate unchanged. Reuse is not independent corroboration.

**Known ambiguity before the fresh application:** sparse boundary descriptions may underdetermine seat; ordinary wording may bundle rule identification with rule application; physical measurements and reports seldom establish causal dependence alone. Section 7's “unless G…” sentence also risks implying an exception to zero phenomenology weight. These are inspected explicitly below. No core distinction was changed before the worker received v0.2.

Close Sense → Enter Shape. The candidate and decision boundary are specific enough for the bounded tests below.
