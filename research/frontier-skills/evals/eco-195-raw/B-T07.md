# B-T07 — indexed-category test of the old-load-evidence claim

**Bounded result:** The bare claim that old load evidence determines *all* destination permission obligations fails. The countermodel below keeps the old-load evidence fixed while destination obligations differ. This tests logical sufficiency under stated assumptions; it says nothing about the actual standing of ECO-194 or any ECOS policy.

## Assumptions and interpretation

1. “Old load evidence” is data indexed by a load context (L), with no destination coordinate encoded in that data.
2. A destination permission obligation can depend on a destination coordinate (D). A permission requirement is represented by a subset of a one-element permission set (G=\{g\}): the empty set means no requirement for (g), and (\{g\}) means (g) is required. This is a deliberately minimal abstraction; it does not say which real-world permission (g) represents.
3. “Determines all” means that, given the old evidence alone and the ordinary reindexing operation, the obligation over (L\times D) is fixed. In particular, without a separate destination rule or input, a load-indexed assignment is pulled back along the projection (p:L\times D\to L). If the claim permits an independent destination-specific policy as an input, that policy is additional evidence and the countermodel does not refute the enriched claim.

## Indexed category and countermodel

Take the base category to be **Set**. The indexed poset of permission obligations is

\[
\mathcal O(X)=(\mathcal P(G))^X,
\qquad f^*(q)=q\circ f \quad(f:Y\to X).
\]

Thus each context (X) has its own fibre of obligation assignments, and reindexing along (f) is substitution. This is an indexed category (a contravariant functor from **Set** to posets): identity substitutions do nothing and ((f\circ h)^*=h^*f^*).

Let (L=\{\ell\}), (D=\{d_0,d_1\}), and let the old-load evidence be the same fixed record (e(\ell)=e_0). No detail of (e_0) is needed. Define an obligation (r\in\mathcal O(L\times D)) by

| Context | Old-load evidence | Permission obligation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ((\ell,d_0)) | (e_0) | (r(\ell,d_0)=\varnothing) |
| ((\ell,d_1)) | (e_0) | (r(\ell,d_1)=\{g\}) |

For every (q\in\mathcal O(L)), its pullback satisfies

\[
(p^*q)(\ell,d_0)=q(\ell)=(p^*q)(\ell,d_1).
\]

So (r\ne p^*q) for every load-indexed (q). In categorical terms, (r) does not descend along the projection (p): it is not constant on the fibre of destinations above the old load. Reindexing the same old-load evidence to both destination contexts cannot generate the displayed difference.

An even weaker reading of “determines” also fails: hold (L,D,e) fixed and consider two admissible assignments (r_A(\ell,d_1)=\varnothing) and (r_B(\ell,d_1)=\{g\}), with the same value at (d_0). If no axiom constrains obligations from (e), both models agree on all old-load evidence and disagree on a destination obligation. Therefore the evidence alone does not logically entail its value.

## What would make the claim hold

A supplied policy could state that every destination obligation has the form (p^*q_e), where (q_e) is uniquely derived from the old evidence; equivalently, obligations are invariant across destinations with the same old load. Alternatively, a specified rule (F(e,d)) could determine obligations using both old evidence and destination data. Neither rule follows from the indexed-category construction itself. Without the governing definitions, records, and policy axioms, this countermodel establishes non-entailment under the stated abstraction, not a finding about any actual ECO-194 permission.
