## Answer

**Evidence floor.** The seed bank has verified only that packet lot IDs and germination records remain associated with the packets across its two local stores. That supports a local identity-and-record transfer claim. It does not establish current seed suitability at the time of distribution, who qualifies to receive seed, whether a recipient has consented, or authority to release a packet. The disaster-response proposal is a new, unqualified handoff. This is a seed-bank design thought experiment, not an assertion about ECOS standing.

**Useful discriminator.** Hold the packet, lot ID and germination record constant. In two proposed releases, vary only the recipient-facing evidence: (A) a current eligibility decision from the program's authorized source plus a documented, valid response for this distribution; (B) no such decision or a declined/missing response. Ask: **Can the distributor obtain and validate both recipient-facing facts independently for each actual handoff?** If yes, prototype the separate gate and audit the link between its decision and the packet release; this still requires the program to define the rules and appropriate consent process. If no, the local transfer proof remains useful for inventory, but the proposed individual release must stay open while those obligations are specified and evidenced. If evidence is partial or disputed, preserve the unresolved branch and test that exact missing fact. This fixture distinguishes stock continuity from recipient qualification without pretending to have performed the test.

The next concrete inquiry is to obtain the proposed distribution protocol and identify its decision authority, eligibility criteria, recipient-information/consent procedure and freshness rules. Only then can a small field fixture tell whether the two-gate mechanism is sufficient, needs another control or should be rejected. No candidate here changes standing or authorizes a distribution.
