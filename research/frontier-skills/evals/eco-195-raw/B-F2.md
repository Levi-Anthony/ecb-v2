# B-F2 — invented museum digitization case

Let (T) mean that the preservation scan and metadata were copied bit-for-bit from the archive to the display server; (P) mean that the archive approved preservation; (L) mean that the separate license for public display has been granted; and (A) mean that public display is authorized. The case supplies (T), (P), and the requirement (A \rightarrow L).

**Candidate claim:** \(\{T, P, A \rightarrow L\} \models A\). This asserts that exact transport of the preservation record, even alongside preservation approval, entails display authorization. The actual consequence of (T) is only that the copied scan and metadata have the same bits as their archive sources. The requirement (A \rightarrow L) supplies a necessary condition for authorization; it does not create a license from a faithful copy.

**Counterexample:** Set (T=\mathrm{true}), (P=\mathrm{true}), (L=\mathrm{false}), and (A=\mathrm{false}). The record arrived unchanged, and preservation was approved, but no display license was granted. Every stated premise is true, including (A \rightarrow L), while the candidate conclusion (A) is false. Thus the entailment fails. The stronger formulation \(T \models A\) fails in the same model.

**Plain result:** Bit-for-bit transport alone does not authorize public display in this invented case.

**What remains open:** Whether a separate display license is later granted, what its terms cover, and whether any other stated conditions for display authorization are satisfied. The stipulated premises do not decide those questions.
