## Raw answer

**Claim tested:** “A change of index for a field survey map transports every relevant obligation.” The word **every** includes obligations that become relevant at the destination; it cannot be narrowed to the successful retrieval of records already indexed at the source. No actual survey map, index convention, governing obligation list, or acceptance rule was supplied. The following definitions and survey policies are my synthetic assumptions, so the result tests the proposed universal inference rather than deciding the standing of a real survey.

**Operational commitment.** If the claim holds merely by changing indices, then an old survey's obligation inventory and its evidence, together with the declared index map, suffice to account for *all* requirements of the revised survey. An audit must be able to identify each revised-map requirement and separately determine whether current evidence satisfies it. A revised map with a newly relevant obligation absent from the old index would falsify the universal assertion.

**Orientation and notation.** Indexed families and change-of-index operations come from set and category theory; their normal job is to organize data by slots and retrieve it along a declared slot map. They do not discover site-specific duties or confer approval. Let `I` be old survey slots and `J` revised slots, and let `t:I→J` identify each old slot with a revised one. Read this as “t, from I to J.” A destination family `B` can be restricted by `t*B=B∘t`, read “t star B equals B composed with t”: it returns the value at `t(i)` for each old slot `i`. The star's direction here is **destination to source**; a bare `t*` is not source-to-destination transport. To give the claim its plausible source-to-destination reading, define direct-image transport explicitly: `(t!A)(j)=⋃_{i∈I:t(i)=j}A(i)`. Read it “t lower shriek A at j is the union of A at all old slots i that t sends to j.” The union over no old slots is the empty set, `∅`. In everyday terms, collect old items under each destination label; a destination label with no old predecessor receives none. This construction and its direction are chosen here, not supplied by the task or established as ECOS semantics.

**Formal consequence.** Write `O_I(i)` for old requirements and `O_J(j)` for *all* relevant revised requirements; write `E_I(i)` for evidence separately. On the declared model, “the change of index transports every relevant obligation” requires at least `O_J=t!O_I` (or an explicitly stated equivalent coverage law). Thus, for every destination slot `j` with no source preimage, it entails `O_J(j)=∅`. Even if this equality holds, `t!E_I` only moves evidence: the validity, currency and applicability of each item at the destination are additional premises before one can say an obligation is satisfied. If `t` is injective and no new requirements arise, the transported old inventory is recovered at its image, but this is only the conditional local transport result.

**Worked counterexample.** Stipulate an old field survey with `I={plot}`. Its sole requirement is `O_I(plot)={boundary-coordinate check}`, supported by an old coordinate-check record in `E_I(plot)`. The revised map has `J={plot′, wetland}`, and `t(plot)=plot′`. Under a stipulated revised-map rule, entry into the `wetland` slot requires `O_J(wetland)={habitat-access clearance}`; no old slot or record addresses it. Direct-image transport gives `(t!O_I)(plot′)={boundary-coordinate check}` and `(t!O_I)(wetland)=∅`, while the actual stipulated `O_J(wetland)` is nonempty. Therefore `O_J≠t!O_I`: one change of index faithfully carries the old requirement yet fails to transport *every* relevant revised obligation. Likewise, the old coordinate-check record at `plot′` cannot satisfy habitat-access clearance at `wetland`. Two revised-map regimes could have identical old-slot restrictions and differ solely in whether habitat clearance is required; inspection of the old view cannot decide between them.

**Translated result and standing.** This is a formal countermodel to the unconditional universal claim under the stated synthetic interpretation. It is not a counterexample to lawful transport of the old survey record, nor proof that any real field survey requires habitat clearance. The useful machinery is scoped restriction/direct-image bookkeeping plus a separate destination obligation-discovery and evidence-validation check. The failure boundary is the inference from *old indexed material is preserved* to *all revised-map requirements have been found and satisfied*. Classify the universal mapping as **REJECTED MAPPING** for this model; classify exact old-slot transport as a **DIRECT FORMALIZATION** only under its declared definitions, with no promotion of actual survey or ECOS standing. For a particular real map, the next discriminator is its authoritative destination requirement inventory and the defined index map: check whether every relevant destination requirement has a qualified source predecessor or an independent destination check, and whether its evidence remains valid there.
