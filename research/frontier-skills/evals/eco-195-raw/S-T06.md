## Trial answer

**Conditional setup.** No governing case packet, source edition, definition of “transport” or “disclosure,” or accepted standing was supplied. For this question only, assume transport means delivering an unchanged, attributable source record into a destination account, while disclosure means identifying and satisfying the destination's applicable obligations, including any new slot the source account did not ask about. These are working definitions for a hypothetical fixture, not claims about an actual ECOS case or authority to use the record.

**Candidate and hinge.** The candidate claim is that successful record transport also establishes complete destination disclosure. Its hinge is *determination*: given the same transported record and valid transport receipt, can destination accounts differ in a required disclosure or its coverage? Preserve the distinction between a receipt for what arrived and a check of what the destination requires.

**Discriminating question.** Can two destination accounts have the same intact, attributable source record and transport receipt, yet one has an applicable, unsatisfied destination disclosure obligation that the other does not? If so, what destination requirement or qualified bridge explains the difference?

**Test (hypothetical, not executed).** Freeze a source record `L`, its hash, provenance, transport rule, receipt, and readability in both destination accounts. In account A, the independently specified destination requirements are `load receipt`; in B they are `load receipt + permission disclosure`. Supply the same `L` and receipt to both, with no permission disclosure in the transported material or separately qualified bridge. Have the destination coverage checker enumerate the applicable requirements from the destination specification and inspect the evidence for each. It should report A's listed slots covered and B's permission slot uncovered. Then add a separately verified permission disclosure to B while holding the transport receipt fixed: B's coverage result should change only after validity and applicability are checked. The test needs the real destination specifications and a qualified interpretation of the slots before it could bear on an actual case.

| Result | Consequence for the next decision |
|---|---|
| **YES:** A and B have the same valid transport evidence but different disclosure coverage. | Reject transport-only sufficiency for this scoped fixture. Keep the local transport result; require an independent destination-obligation inventory and coverage/authorization check before relying on B. |
| **NO, under a complete and qualified bridge:** every applicable destination disclosure is demonstrably determined and covered by the transported evidence. | The receipt may support disclosure coverage within that bridge's proven scope. Audit its completeness, applicability, currentness, and validity before any use; one failed search for a counterexample does not establish the bridge. |
| **Underdetermined:** obligation inventories, slot meanings, or evidence validity are unknown. | Hold the sufficiency claim open. Recover the destination specification and bridge/evidence, then rerun the fixture. |

**Scoped standing.** This is a proposed discriminator and test design, not a result of an executed test. It neither changes accepted standing nor grants current-use authority.
