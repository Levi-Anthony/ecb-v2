# Record transport and new access obligations

The projection change has one verified invariant: stop identifiers and arrival timestamps survive exactly. That supports carrying those two fields into the new map representation. It does not establish wheelchair access for any route proposed by the new planner; that question requires a separate on-site assessment. These are different claims with different evidence.

| Structural connection | Decision payoff | Discriminator for the next inquiry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Invariant-preserving transformation.** Treat the projection change as a transformation of representation. The verified identifiers and timestamps are invariants of that transformation. | Reuse those fields for record matching and timing without repeating the same verification solely because the map projection changed. | Compare each source record with its transformed record for identifier and timestamp equality. Ask separately whether any other field relevant to access was transformed or inferred; no such preservation is stipulated. |
| **A join is not an access finding.** A planner can join stops and arrival times into a candidate itinerary, but an itinerary assembled from correct records does not inherit a wheelchair-access conclusion. | Keep candidate generation available while withholding an access claim about the candidate route. | For every proposed route, identify the exact access claim being made and the on-site observation that would support it. If the claim rests only on joined identifiers and times, the evidence does not discriminate accessible from inaccessible routes. |
| **Different proof obligations at a system boundary.** Exact record transport answers whether two representations refer to the same stop and time. Wheelchair access answers whether a person can use the route in the relevant physical setting. | Assign the next work to the missing obligation instead of treating a data migration check as a route acceptance test. | Inspect the evidence attached to each claim: a field equality check satisfies transport; a route-specific on-site assessment is needed for access. If that assessment has not occurred, the access obligation remains open. |
| **Provenance can travel farther than assurance.** The planner may preserve where its stop and time facts came from while producing a new route recommendation. The provenance of inputs does not validate the recommendation's new physical-world property. | Report reliable itinerary inputs and unresolved access status side by side, so users of the analysis can see the precise boundary of confidence. | Trace a proposed route back to its stop and time records, then ask whether there is independently collected, route-specific access evidence. The former trace cannot substitute for the latter. |

The practical next step is to define the route-specific wheelchair-access question and conduct the stipulated on-site assessment for candidate routes. Until then, the evidence supports exact transport of stop identifiers and arrival timestamps through the projection change, and no route approval on wheelchair-access grounds.
