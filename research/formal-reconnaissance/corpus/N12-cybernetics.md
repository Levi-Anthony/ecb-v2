### R089 — Ashby 1956 (requisite variety)
`CITE` W. R. Ashby. *An Introduction to Cybernetics.* Chapman & Hall, 1956 (Law of Requisite Variety, ch. 11). · **HIGH**
`PROBLEM` What capacity must a regulator have to hold an outcome within limits?
`STRUCTURE` **Only variety can destroy variety**: the regulator's variety must be at least the disturbance variety divided by the outcome variety. A counting argument over a table of outcomes.
`GUARANTEE` A genuine lower bound. It is a theorem, not a slogan — but a theorem about a specific finite game.
`LOSS` Outcomes outside the regulator's reach are simply unregulable.
`DETECTOR` Disturbance variety exceeds regulatory capacity; the outcome escapes.
`REOPEN` Increase regulator variety, or **reduce disturbance variety** — the second option is the more useful one.
`SEAM` S-5, S-2
`INTERNAL` Master Key bounding present relevance; the human rail as a finite-capacity regulator.
`DELTA` Supplies a real bound with a genuinely useful corollary. The human rail has bounded variety; if consequential transitions requiring human adjudication grow faster than that, governance provably fails. The Master Key's function is then not only epistemic but **regulatory**: by bounding relevance it reduces the disturbance variety the human must absorb. That reframing is decision-useful — it says relevance bounding and human-rail sustainability are the same problem.
`TRANSFER` Track the rate of transitions requiring human adjudication as a capacity measure. If it grows without bound, either the Master Key is not bounding enough or the enforcement classification is pushing too much to the AUTHORITY surface.
`PREREQ` Countable disturbance and regulator variety. In v2 this is a rate count of human-gated decisions — measurable, and ECB already logs them.
`PRESERVE` `capability ≠ warrant` — regulatory capacity is not authority.
`FALSIFIER` Human adjudication load stays bounded regardless of system growth — which would mean something else is already doing the bounding, and it would be worth identifying.
`BRANCH` BRANCH_NEUTRAL
`ENFORCE` OBSERVATIONAL
`COST` NONE
`RETURN` Yes — capacity saturation is returnable evidence.
`STATUS` TESTABLE_TRANSFER
`EFFECT` SUPPLIES_TEST
`BUILD` ADD_ACCEPTANCE_TEST
`PRIORITY` 2

### R090 — Conant & Ashby 1970 (every good regulator)
`CITE` R. C. Conant, W. R. Ashby. *Every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system.* International Journal of Systems Science 1(2), 89–97, 1970. · **HIGH**
`PROBLEM` Must a successful regulator embody a model of what it regulates?
`STRUCTURE` A theorem: an optimal, error-free regulator is necessarily a homomorphic image of the regulated system — **under strong assumptions** (optimality, determinism, a specific error measure).
`GUARANTEE` The stated implication, within those assumptions.
`LOSS` The generality the popular slogan implies.
`DETECTOR` A regulator succeeding without a model — which merely shows an assumption failed.
`REOPEN` Check which assumption failed.
`SEAM` S-2, S-7
`INTERNAL` Self-representation; the governing documents as a model of the system.
`DELTA` **Retained mainly as a guardrail against its own popular misuse.** The theorem is real but far narrower than the slogan "you must model what you govern"; it assumes optimality and determinism, neither of which v2 has. It would be easy — and wrong — to cite it as proof that v2's self-representation is necessary. The genuine v2 argument for a self-model is Maes's causal connection (R088), which requires no optimality assumption, not this theorem.
`TRANSFER` **None.** Use R088 for the self-model argument. Do not cite Conant–Ashby as licensing v2's self-representation.
`PREREQ` Optimality and determinism — absent.
`PRESERVE` §12; §21's prohibition on promoting analogy into formal equivalence.
`FALSIFIER` v2 governance shown optimal and deterministic in the required sense. It is neither.
`BRANCH` BRANCH_DEPENDENT (prerequisites absent)
`ENFORCE` —
`COST` NONE
`RETURN` None.
`STATUS` METAPHOR_ONLY
`EFFECT` CHALLENGES_EXISTING (challenges a tempting citation)
`BUILD` NO_BUILD_EFFECT
`PRIORITY` 1

### R091 — Beer 1979 (Viable System Model)
`CITE` S. Beer. *The Heart of Enterprise.* Wiley, 1979 (and *Brain of the Firm*, 1972). · **HIGH**
`PROBLEM` What organizational structure is necessary for a system to remain viable?
`STRUCTURE` Five recursively nested systems (operations, coordination, control, intelligence, policy), each viable system containing and contained in others.
`GUARANTEE` None that is formally checkable. VSM is a diagnostic framework.
`LOSS` n/a.
`DETECTOR` VSM identifies missing functions diagnostically, not by proof.
`REOPEN` n/a.
`SEAM` S-7
`INTERNAL` Holarchy / compositional-unit language in the ECB Charter's first-principles base layer.
`DELTA` The recursive-organization framing resembles v2's self-hosting closely enough to be attractive, and it generates **no proof obligation, no failure detector, and no falsifier**. Under §12 that is `METAPHOR_ONLY` by construction. Recorded explicitly because the ECB Charter already uses holarchic vocabulary, so the temptation to promote VSM to architecture is live rather than hypothetical.
`TRANSFER` None. VSM may be used as descriptive vocabulary; it may not generate an invariant, a test, or a structure.
`PREREQ` n/a — no formal content to instantiate.
`PRESERVE` §12; structure-is-earned.
`FALSIFIER` n/a — the absence of a falsifier is precisely the disposition.
