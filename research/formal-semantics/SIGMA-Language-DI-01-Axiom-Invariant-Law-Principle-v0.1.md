# SIGMA Language D&I 01 — Axiom / Invariant / Law / Principle

**Date:** 6 October 2026, America/Phoenix  
**Standing:** Direct Differentiate-and-Integrate pass over current SIGMA/ECOS language.  
**Scope:** Current-facing theory and architecture vocabulary. Historical source wording remains provenance.  
**Controlling method:** simplify access, not structure; shared wording never proves shared semantics.

## 1. Commission

Determine whether **axiom**, **invariant**, **law**, and **principle** are genuinely sibling categories.

For each term:

1. recover its characteristic work from live SIGMA/ECOS usage;
2. distinguish nearest neighbors;
3. identify the question that selects the term;
4. wire a discriminating sensor;
5. test hostile substitutions on current fixtures;
6. integrate the surviving relations;
7. return explicit prospective-language and D&I standing.

## 2. Controlling finding

**FAIL the proposed one-axis family.**

Axiom, invariant, law, and principle do not answer the same question.

At least three independent classification dimensions are present:

### D1 — derivation standing

**AXIOM ↔ DERIVED RESULT / THEOREM**

Question: **Where does this claim sit in the derivation?**

An axiom is an explicit starting commitment of the instrument/theory at a declared scope. It is not counted as derived from lower commitments inside that same derivation.

A theorem or other derived result follows from installed premises, definitions, operators, evidence, or contracts.

This dimension is orthogonal to invocation type. A claim may be both an axiom and an invariant.

### D2 — invocation / compliance contract

**INVARIANT ↔ PRINCIPLE**

Question: **How does the claim govern use?**

An invariant is **checked**. Within its declared transformation/use class, it must hold. A violation means the transformation/realization is invalid under that contract, or the scope/contract must be reopened.

A principle is **heeded**. It shapes judgment, selection, or design but may be appealed past in a particular case without thereby leaving the practice, provided the departure is explicit and warranted.

This recovers the prior invocation-contract work and the Rawls practice-vs-summary discriminator.

### D3 — relational form

**LAW**

Question: **What relation does the claim assert among conditions, variables, transformations, or consequences?**

A law states a scoped relation such that, when its antecedent conditions obtain, specified consequences, dependencies, permitted transitions, or prohibited collapses follow.

A law may be axiomatic or derived.

A law may entail one or more invariants.

A law is not automatically a principle and is not automatically an invariant.

The current project therefore should not use **law** as merely “very important rule.”

## 3. AXIOM

### Plain entry

An axiom is where the current derivation begins.

### Technical identity

An **axiom** is an explicit starting stipulation or commitment that is not derived from lower commitments within the same declared instrument/theory scope.

This is **derivation standing**, not an invocation type.

### What it does

- establishes the starting basis from which derivation may proceed;
- makes responsibility for an unproved starting commitment explicit;
- prevents derived results from being smuggled back into the premise set.

### What it does not do

An axiom does not automatically mean:

- invariant;
- universal metaphysical truth;
- empirically verified fact;
- principle;
- law;
- highest-importance claim.

### Sensor

Ask:

> **If this statement is removed while the remaining premises and operators are held fixed, can the statement itself be re-derived without circularity?**

- **Yes:** it is not independent as an axiom in this derivation.
- **No, and the instrument explicitly requires it as a starting commitment:** axiom standing is available.
- **Unknown:** axiom standing remains open.

Then ask:

> **Is the claim being asserted as a starting commitment, or merely behaving broadly because it is a high-level derived result?**

Breadth is not axiomhood.

### Hostile substitution

**Quadrant co-arising** can be installed as an instrumental axiom even while its downstream preservation obligations may generate invariants.

Calling every Quadrant-derived constraint an “axiom” would erase derivation depth.

### Current D&I standing

**D&I-QUALIFIED as a derivation-standing term.**

## 4. INVARIANT

### Plain entry

An invariant names what must continue to hold across a declared class of change.

### Technical identity

An **invariant** is a property, distinction, relation, or constraint that must be preserved or satisfied across a specified transformation, implementation, traversal, or use class.

It is **checked**, not merely admired or encouraged.

### What it does

- defines validity across change;
- supplies a violation surface;
- allows implementations/transformations to vary while preserving required structure;
- supports negative controls and property tests.

### Required contract

An invariant is incomplete unless it names:

1. the **subject**;
2. the **transformation/use class** across which it must hold;
3. the **property/relation** that must remain held;
4. a **violation sensor**;
5. the consequence of violation: reject, HOLD, reopen, or requalify.

### Sensor

Ask:

> **Across what exact class of change must this continue to hold?**

Then:

> **What observable or attributable result would count as a violation?**

Then:

> **May the worker knowingly override it while remaining inside the same valid practice/contract?**

- **No:** invariant is supported.
- **Yes, with case-specific judgment:** likely principle rather than invariant.
- **No transformation class or violation condition can be named:** invariant standing is not earned.

### Hostile substitution

The historical **Two-Door Principle** is an invariant under the invocation test:

> Does the relevant surface preserve both required access doors under the declared scope?

That is checked and can be violated. It is not merely heeded.

### Current D&I standing

**D&I-QUALIFIED as an invocation/preservation term.**

## 5. PRINCIPLE

### Plain entry

A principle shapes judgment without defining every valid instance of the practice.

### Technical identity

A **principle** is a general orienting or design rule that should be heeded, but is defeasible within its declared scope.

A warranted exception does not automatically invalidate the practice itself.

### What it does

- guides selection among otherwise legitimate options;
- expresses default preference or direction;
- carries reusable reasoning without pretending to mechanical necessity;
- can motivate more specific contracts, rules, or invariants.

### What it does not do

A principle does not automatically:

- create a binary held/violated test;
- establish a premise of the theory;
- state a necessary relation among variables;
- confer authority or standing.

### Sensor

Ask:

> **Can a competent participant knowingly depart from this rule in a named case and still remain within the same valid practice?**

- **Yes, if the departure is explicit and warranted:** principle.
- **No; departure makes the realization invalid under the same contract:** invariant or requirement, not principle.

Then ask:

> **Is the rule guiding judgment, or does it claim a necessary relation?**

If it claims a necessary relation under antecedent conditions, inspect for **law**.

### Hostile substitution

The **correspondence/subsumption principle** may be principle-shaped at the redesign-strategy level, but any descendant gate such as “nothing is silently dropped” may be an invariant/requirement.

Do not let one label propagate automatically from source principle to every implementation tooth.

### Current D&I standing

**D&I-QUALIFIED as a defeasible invocation/guidance term.**

## 6. LAW

### Plain entry

A law says what follows when stated conditions obtain.

### Technical identity

A **law** is a scoped relational claim connecting antecedent conditions, variables, transformations, and consequences strongly enough that counterexample, contradiction, or scope failure can defeat the law.

Within SIGMA this includes **instrumental/formal laws**. The term does not imply a natural-science law or metaphysical necessity.

### What it does

- compresses multiple lower rules into a relation;
- supports derivation and prediction of consequences;
- states how a system/inquiry must behave under declared conditions;
- may generate invariants for particular transformations.

### What it does not do

A law is not:

- an axiom merely because it is foundational;
- an invariant merely because violations are disallowed;
- a principle merely because it guides work;
- a rhetorical honorific for an important sentence.

### Sensor

Ask:

> **What are the antecedent conditions?**

Then:

> **What relation, consequence, dependency, or transition follows if those conditions obtain?**

Then:

> **What counterexample would falsify or narrow the claim rather than merely count as noncompliance?**

If no antecedent/consequent relation and no possible counterexample/scope failure can be stated, **law** is not earned.

### Integration with axiom

A law can be **axiomatic** if the relational claim is installed as a starting commitment.

A law can be **derived** if it follows from prior axioms/contracts.

These are orthogonal descriptions.

### Integration with invariant

A law can imply invariants.

Example form:

> If a lawful reorientation changes G while R/B are held fixed, R/B do not change merely because G changed.

The relational claim may be described as an independence law.

The preserved coordinates under `Reorient` are invariants of that transformation.

### Integration with principle

A principle can guide adoption or application of a law.

A law does not become defeasible merely because a principle motivated its discovery.

### Current D&I standing

**D&I-QUALIFIED as a relational-form term, with a strict sensor.**

## 7. Real-fixture substitution tests

### Fixture A — Two-Door

Historical label: **Two-Door Principle**.

Invocation:
> Does the declared surface preserve both required access doors?

A negative answer is a violation, not a discretionary exception.

**D&I result:** invariant.

Prospective language:
- **PREFERRED:** Two-Door Invariant.
- **PREDECESSOR / exact historical name:** Two-Door Principle.
- Historical artifact keys/titles remain exact provenance unless separately migrated.

### Fixture B — Holonic / Quadrant starting commitments

The current SIGMA theory intentionally treats holonic and fourfold Quadrant commitments as work-yielding instrumental starting stipulations rather than metaphysical proof claims.

**D&I result:** axiom standing is appropriate at the declared SIGMA instrument scope.

Derived constraints about traversal, referent preservation, coverage, and non-reduction must not be relabeled axioms merely because they descend from those axioms.

### Fixture C — local closure without global foreclosure

Binding Infinity supplies the governing problem:

- inquiry remains indefinitely extensible;
- finite work requires PGO-relative sufficiency and stopping;
- local closure must not entail exhaustion of the remaining field.

The phrase **local closure without global foreclosure** states a conditional governing relation, not merely advice.

**D&I result:** **law** is supported as relational form.

A derived implementation invariant may then require that closure/compression preserve the reentry/recovery handles required by the current scope.

Do not merge the law with that invariant.

### Fixture D — PGO non-manufacture

Claim:
> PGO may change relevance, required resolution, sufficiency, and reentry without by itself manufacturing R identity, constituency, evidence, truth/currentness, Type, authority, or permission.

This is most directly tested across `Reorient`.

**D&I result:** primary standing is **invariant / coordinate-independence constraint across Reorient**, not “law” merely because it is important.

A broader independence law may be derived over the tri-axial coordinate system, but **PGO non-manufacture invariant** is the preferred current label for the enforcement tooth.

### Fixture E — two-part reconciliation

`Reconcile = CheckAffected(Affected_old(delta)) AND CheckCoverage(Req(new_basis,use), Accounted(new_basis,use))`

This states what a lawful material/consequential change requires.

**D&I result:** **law** is supported as relational form.

Its implementation may contain invariant gates requiring that neither side of the reconciliation be silently omitted.

### Fixture F — “current ≠ newest”

This is a preserved independence relation across temporal ordering/currentness selection.

**D&I result:** **invariant** at the currentness architecture seat.

Calling it a principle would incorrectly suggest that newest may sometimes become current by appeal alone.

Calling it a law adds no useful relational content unless embedded in a broader currentness law.

## 8. Integration result

The old flat family is superseded.

Use a multi-axis classification.

### Derivation standing
- axiom;
- derived result / theorem.

### Invocation contract
- invariant;
- principle;
- requirement/obligation/rule families to be D&I'd separately.

### Relational form
- law;
- definition;
- dependency relation;
- equivalence;
- other forms to be classified as needed.

A single claim may legitimately be:

- **axiomatic + invariant**;
- **derived + invariant**;
- **derived + law**;
- **axiomatic + law**;
- **principle + derived**.

Do not force one label to exclude orthogonal labels.

## 9. Sensors to install in authoring/review

### Axiom sensor
> Can this be derived from the remaining installed commitments without circularity? If not, is it explicitly accepted as a starting commitment at this scope?

### Invariant sensor
> Across what exact transformation/use class must this hold, and what result would count as violation?

### Principle sensor
> May a competent participant depart from this in a warranted case while remaining inside the same valid practice?

### Law sensor
> Under what antecedent conditions does what relation or consequence follow, and what counterexample would falsify or narrow it?

If the relevant sensor cannot be answered, do not use the label as a confidence adjective.

## 10. Supersession / language outputs

1. **SUPERSEDE** the single-axis claim-status row that treated axiom, invariant, law, and principle as siblings.
2. **RETAIN** all four terms, but on distinct dimensions.
3. **PREFER** “Two-Door Invariant” for prospective theory language; preserve “Two-Door Principle” as exact historical/provenance language.
4. **PREFER** “PGO non-manufacture invariant” for the enforcement claim across Reorient.
5. **RETAIN** “local closure without global foreclosure” as a governing law of Binding Infinity, while separately deriving closure/recoverability invariants.
6. **RETAIN** “two-part reconciliation law” as relational form; separately name its implementation invariants.
7. **PROHIBIT** using axiom / invariant / law / principle as escalating honorifics.
